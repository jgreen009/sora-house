# Sora House — creative direction

Sora House is a twelve-room hotel on Portugal's Atlantic coast. The site should feel architectural, warm, and unhurried. Space, proportion, natural materials, and daylight carry the identity; interface decoration stays secondary.

## Visual language

Instrument Serif gives headings and prices an editorial voice, while DM Sans keeps navigation, room facts, and forms clear. Ivory is the default surface, sandstone introduces a warmer pause, and coastal green provides one dark feature and the footer. Hairline rules repeat the horizon motif. Media stays rectangular, with no resort-style ornament or urgency badges.

Layouts vary by purpose. The home page moves from a large atmospheric introduction to practical room choice, material details, and coastal context. Room pages introduce the name and rate before the photographs, then provide a clear path into dates and room facts. Comparison becomes a straightforward card layout on narrow screens rather than hiding the decision-making details offscreen.

## Photography

The selected Higgsfield imagery shares pale stone, deep arches, warm oak, washed linen, dune grass, and an Atlantic horizon. Primary images distinguish the four categories by outlook and spatial arrangement. Secondary room views use corrected references to maintain the same construction and furnishings. Material close-ups provide changes of scale between larger architectural scenes.

Preserve original masters and generation provenance internally. Use responsive web derivatives in the site, verify important crops, and avoid claiming that the imagery came from an on-location shoot. Keep the house recognisable across all pages.

## Motion and behaviour

Motion establishes reading order and gives clear feedback: coordinated entrances, shallow image reveals, measured gallery reflow, menu staging, and focused planning steps. Keep native scrolling, predictable controls, visible focus, and an immediate reduced-motion path. The user should always be able to find room facts, price, next action, and the way back.

## Voice

Use specific British English and calm invitations. Describe the room and the action accurately. Do not invent opening hours, staff, testimonials, awards, or completed transactions. Keep service-availability messages brief and factual.
