## Library continuity and implementation guidance

The palette and photographic treatment are consistent: pale porous limestone, muted plaster, honey-toned timber, unbleached linen, low coastal grasses, grey-blue sea, and warm daylight. The library looks related to the reviewed house exterior rather than a collection of unrelated hotels. Room hierarchy is visible through outlook and spatial arrangement: Dune is compact and dune-facing; Courtyard looks into the garden; Atlantic makes the sea opening dominant; Suite includes a separate sitting area and hearth.

The main weakness is exact scene reconstruction across paired room images. The existing category counts permit more than one Dune, Courtyard, or Atlantic room, but no evidence proves that these variants correspond to a specific real room. General editorial placements can use the detail assets without naming an exact room. The single Suite warrants particular care: 04 and 08 should not form a claimed exact-suite photo sequence while furniture continuity remains unresolved.

Use 01–04 as category primaries. Use material close-ups, the coast, bath, dining table, and architectural views for supporting editorial sections. Verify all cover crops and text contrast in rendered pages: room assets contain several competing focal points and are generally better beside text than underneath large headings. The main room images should remain distinct when reduced to cards. Exact room areas, counts, capacity, and rates must still come from `content/room-data/rooms.json`.

No asset replacement is demanded by an obvious visual defect at this inspection scale. Exact-room galleries that require matched views need a continuity correction or a deliberate change of placement before being treated as approved.
